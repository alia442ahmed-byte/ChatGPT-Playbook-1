You ask it something.
It has no idea who you are.
So it guesses.

That's why most answers feel generic. The business owners getting real hours back never let it guess. They run six moves first. Each one takes three seconds.

six moves below
⌄
1
Turn this off first.
Three clicks. After this, nothing you type trains their model.
Settings
→
Data Controls
→
Toggle: OFF
2
Introduce yourself. Once.
One prompt. It stops guessing who you are and starts working like it's on your payroll.
Paste This
I run a business and I want you to build a Master Prompt: one document that tells you everything about who I am and how my business works, so I never explain it again. Ask me one question at a time about what I sell and how I price it, who my ideal client is, how my team runs day to day, and what's slowing me down right now. Once I've answered everything, write it into one clean document I can save and reuse. Ask me the first question.
3
Set the tone. Permanently.
Paste this once. Every answer after this sounds like a person who knows you, not a search bar.

Paste This
Keep answers short and easy to scan. Use bullet points only when I ask for a list. Skip the preamble and give me the answer first. Don't say "Certainly" or "I hope this helps." If you're missing information, ask me one direct question instead of guessing.
4
This isn't just for writing.
Get one output exactly right, a write-up, a build, a research pass, then ask for the recipe behind it. Reuse it forever.
Paste This
This is exactly how I want it. Write the system prompt that would have produced this output from the start, so I can reuse it any time.

Stop re-explaining your business.
One folder. Upload your context once, reference it in every chat after.
